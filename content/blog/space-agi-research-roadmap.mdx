# Space AGI Research Roadmap: From Lab to Orbit

Space AGI is transitioning from research to deployment. Collision avoidance, autonomous rovers, and formation flying are already in orbit. The next decade will see more capable systems—lunar construction robots, Mars helicopter swarms, and deep-space probes. This article outlines a research roadmap: near-term milestones, key technical challenges, and the path from simulation to operational space systems.

## Near-Term (2026–2030): Maturity and Scale

**Collision avoidance** will become standard for all large constellations. AI-based systems will replace rule-based approaches, improving fuel efficiency and safety. **Rover autonomy** will expand: Perseverance-style AutoNav will be baseline; science autonomy—choosing targets and sampling strategies—will mature. **Formation flying** will deploy for science missions (interferometry, distributed sensing) and commercial applications. Research focus: sim-to-real transfer, verification and validation, and human-AI teaming for mixed autonomy.

## Mid-Term (2030–2035): Lunar and Cislunar

**Lunar construction** will move from demos to operational systems. Robots will excavate, print, and assemble. Multi-agent coordination will be critical. **Cislunar operations**—the space between Earth and Moon—will host the Lunar Gateway, fuel depots, and commercial stations. AI will manage traffic, docking, and resource allocation. Research focus: multi-robot systems, long-duration autonomy, and resilience to communication outages.

## Long-Term (2035–2040): Mars and Beyond

**Mars surface operations** will scale: multiple rovers, helicopters, and eventually human-support robots. AI will handle coordination, science prioritization, and infrastructure maintenance. **Deep-space probes** will carry more autonomy—fault tolerance, science prioritization, and perhaps limited self-modification. Research focus: decades-long reliability, novel learning paradigms (e.g., continual learning), and human-AI collaboration at interplanetary distances.

## Key Technical Challenges

**Verification and assurance:** How do we prove an AI system is safe? Formal methods, testing, and monitoring will all play a role. **Sim-to-real:** Simulation is never perfect. Domain adaptation, system identification, and robust training are active research areas. **Scaling:** Current RL and planning algorithms struggle with high-dimensional state and action spaces. Better representations, hierarchical methods, and hybrid classical-AI approaches are needed. **Energy and compute:** Spacecraft have strict power budgets. Efficient algorithms and hardware (e.g., neuromorphic chips) will be essential.

## Research Priorities

1. **Robust perception** in challenging environments (dust, low light, occlusion)
2. **Sample-efficient learning** for data-limited domains
3. **Multi-agent coordination** with limited communication
4. **Fault tolerance** and self-healing
5. **Verification** of AI systems for safety-critical applications

## Key Concepts

- **Sim-to-real** = Transferring learned behavior from simulation to hardware
- **Verification** = Proving correctness or safety of AI systems
- **Cislunar** = Space between Earth and Moon
- **Human-AI teaming** = Mixed autonomy with human oversight
- **Continual learning** = Learning without forgetting over long missions

## Why It Matters

Space AGI is not a single technology—it is a convergence of perception, planning, control, and learning. The roadmap connects today's research to tomorrow's missions. Engineers, students, and researchers who understand this trajectory can contribute to the most exciting engineering challenge of the century: extending human presence beyond Earth with intelligent machines.
